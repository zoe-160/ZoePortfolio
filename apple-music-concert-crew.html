01

The idea changed once I looked closer at the problem.

Apple Music already helps people find concerts and get to ticketing, so I did not want to design a feature around something the product could already do. I became more interested in what happens after someone finds a show and realizes their friends might want to go too.

That part of the process can easily turn into separate texts, screenshots, ticket links, conversations about budget, and the same question being asked more than once: who is actually going? I decided to focus the project on making that part of planning feel easier instead of redesigning concert discovery itself.

The question I ended up designing around was: how can Apple Music help friends turn a shared interest in a concert into an actual plan to go together?

02

How the idea changed

This project actually started as a Spotify × Ticketmaster concept. At first I was trying to improve concert discovery, planning, and ticketing all at once. The more I looked at what Spotify and other music platforms already offered, the more I realized that discovery was not the part of the idea that felt most useful or original.

I shifted the project to Apple Music and focused on the social part between finding a concert and buying a ticket. That also made the boundaries of the feature clearer to me. Apple Music could help people find the show and organize the plan, Messages could still handle a real conversation, and Ticketmaster could continue handling the actual ticket purchase.

03

From finding the show to making the plan

I wanted Concert Crew to feel like a natural extension of Apple Music instead of a separate event-planning app, so the experience starts inside concert discovery and only brings in the social features when they become useful.

04

Building the flow in Figma Make

I built the concept in Figma Make because I wanted to see how the idea felt as an actual flow instead of only designing separate screens. I was able to move from concert discovery into the crew, change preferences, invite a friend, see the summary update, and continue toward tickets.

Using an AI-assisted prototyping tool made it faster for me to get the idea working, but I still had to make decisions about what the feature actually needed. A lot of my time went into deciding what belonged inside Concert Crew, what should stay in Messages, and where Apple Music should stop and let Ticketmaster take over.

05

What I learned from the pivot

The biggest improvement I made to this project was not adding another screen. It was realizing that I was trying to solve too much at once.

Once I narrowed the project down to planning with friends, the rest of the experience became much easier to make decisions about. Concert discovery could stay familiar, and Concert Crew finally had a clear reason to exist beyond being another place to look at shows.

If I continued developing this concept, I would want to learn which planning details people actually care about most, whether they are comfortable letting friends see that they are interested in a show, and when a group feels ready enough to move from talking about a concert to actually looking for tickets.
