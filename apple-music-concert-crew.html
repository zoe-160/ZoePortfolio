01

Planning a concert often means moving across several apps.

As someone who goes to concerts often, I have found that discovering a show is only one part of the process. Planning with friends usually means moving between music, ticketing, and messaging apps.

A concert may be discovered through Apple Music, while dates and prices are checked through Ticketmaster and the planning itself happens through text messages. The same information is often repeated or searched more than once.

Because Apple Music already includes friends and concert discovery, I wanted to explore how those existing features could support planning. If two friends share interest in an artist, the app could provide a clear way to signal interest and coordinate the basic details of attending together.

The question I designed around was: how can Apple Music help friends turn shared interest in a concert into a coordinated plan?

SHARED INTERESTMake it easier to notice when a friend might want to attend the same show.
COORDINATIONKeep attendance, budget, dates, and seating preferences together instead of spreading them across texts and ticket pages.
COMMITMENTProvide a clear transition from planning into ticket selection.
02

Defining the role of Concert Crew

Concert Crew was designed to support planning without replacing Messages or Ticketmaster. Its role is to organize the information friends need while deciding whether a concert works for the group.

That required defining what Apple Music should surface, what should remain optional, and how the interface should represent differences in attendance, budget, and seating preferences.

01Interest is explicit. Friends appear as Interested or Going only after choosing that status or joining the Concert Crew; listening history alone does not determine participation.
02Different preferences remain visible. The crew summary can show a budget range and multiple seating preferences without assuming the group has reached agreement.
03Apple Music supports planning, Messages remains available for conversation, and Ticketmaster remains responsible for live inventory and checkout.
04Shared artist interest can prompt a nudge or invite, while joining the crew remains an intentional action.
03

From finding the show to making the plan

Concert Crew needed to feel like an extension of Apple Music rather than a separate planning service. The flow begins within concert discovery and introduces social planning only when it becomes relevant.

04

Building the flow in Figma Make

Figma Make allowed me to test the concept as a connected interaction flow rather than a set of static screens. The prototype moves from concert discovery into Concert Crew, supports preference changes and invitations, updates the group summary, and continues into ticket selection.

AI-assisted prototyping accelerated the interaction work, while the core design decisions remained focused on scope: what belongs in Concert Crew, what should remain in Messages, and where Apple Music should hand off to Ticketmaster.

05

What I learned

Focusing the concept on the planning stage, rather than adding more concert-discovery functionality, gave the project a clearer purpose.

Defining a clear role for each product helped keep the concept focused. Apple Music supports discovery and coordination, Messages remains available for conversation, and Ticketmaster handles live ticket inventory and checkout.

If I continued developing the concept, I would test which planning details matter most, how comfortable users are sharing concert interest with friends, and what information helps a group feel ready to move into ticket selection.
