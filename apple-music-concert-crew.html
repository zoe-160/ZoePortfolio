01

The idea came from how I already plan concerts with friends.

As someone who goes to concerts often, finding the show is usually the easy part. Planning it with friends is where everything starts getting split between apps.

A concert might come up through Apple Music, but dates and prices are checked separately through Ticketmaster, and then the actual planning happens through text messages. Someone sends a screenshot, someone else checks a different section, and the same basic information gets searched more than once.

Apple Music already has a friend system and concert discovery, which made me curious about what it could look like if those two things worked together. If a concert appears for an artist two friends already listen to, one person could nudge the other and keep the early planning in one place.

The question I designed around became: how can Apple Music help friends turn a shared interest in a concert into an actual plan to go together?

SHARED INTERESTMake it easier to notice when a friend might want to attend the same show.
COORDINATIONKeep attendance, budget, dates, and seating preferences together instead of spreading them across texts and ticket pages.
COMMITMENTGive the group a clear point to move from talking about the show into looking for tickets.
02

Keeping the planning useful without making Apple Music do everything

The goal was never to replace Messages or Ticketmaster. Concert Crew is meant to hold the information friends need while they are deciding whether a show actually works for the group.

That meant being specific about what Apple Music should show, what needs to stay optional, and what happens when everyone does not agree right away.

01Interest is explicit. A friend appears as Interested or Going after choosing that status or joining the Concert Crew, rather than Apple Music silently assuming it from listening history.
02Different preferences stay visible. The crew summary can show a budget range and multiple seating preferences instead of pretending the group has already agreed.
03Apple Music supports the plan, Messages can still handle a longer conversation, and Ticketmaster keeps the actual inventory and checkout.
04A shared artist interest creates the opening for a nudge or invite, but the decision to join the crew still belongs to the friend.
03

From finding the show to making the plan

Concert Crew needed to feel like a natural extension of Apple Music instead of a separate event-planning app. The experience starts inside concert discovery and only brings in the social features when they become useful.

04

Building the flow in Figma Make

Figma Make gave me a way to see how the idea felt as an actual flow instead of a set of separate screens. The prototype moves from concert discovery into the crew, lets preferences change, supports inviting a friend, updates the group summary, and continues toward tickets.

The speed of an AI-assisted prototyping tool made it easier to test the full idea, but the product decisions still mattered most. Much of the process came down to deciding what belonged inside Concert Crew, what should stay in Messages, and where Apple Music should stop and let Ticketmaster take over.

05

What this project taught me

The strongest part of this project became the planning itself, not adding more concert-discovery features.

Giving each product a clear role made the experience easier to think through. Apple Music can surface the show and help friends organize the plan, while Messages and Ticketmaster continue doing the parts they already do well.

If I continued developing this concept, I would want to learn which planning details people actually care about most, whether they are comfortable letting friends see that they are interested in a show, and when a group feels ready enough to move from talking about a concert to actually looking for tickets.
