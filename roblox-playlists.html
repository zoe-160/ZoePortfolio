01

The problem: choosing what to play is often a group decision.

When friends want to play Roblox together, the group may spend time searching separately, sharing suggestions, and deciding which experience fits the mood before anyone starts playing.

Roblox Playlists extends the idea of saved experiences beyond personal Favorites. A player could create themed collections—such as Horror Nights, Fashion, or Friday Night Favorites—and choose whether to keep them private or build them with friends.

The distinction is not simply another way to bookmark games. Playlists organize experiences around a purpose or occasion and can support group contribution and decision-making when those features are useful.

The question I designed around was: how can friends collect game ideas and decide what to play together without adding unnecessary friction to a casual play session?

COLLECTGive game suggestions a shared home instead of scattering them across search and conversation.
DECIDEMake voting and play order visible so the group can make a decision efficiently.
PLAYConnect the selected experience to an existing Party rather than duplicating Roblox’s existing social features.
02

Designing the playlist to work individually or collaboratively

The playlist needed to remain useful even when no collaboration was required. A user can start privately, save experiences, and later share the collection if friends want to contribute.

Once shared, friends can add suggestions, see who contributed each game, vote when needed, and reorder the collection. Collaboration is optional so the feature can support both personal organization and group planning.

01Start with a familiar collection model: create, name, save, and organize.
02Add collaboration as an optional state rather than making every playlist shared by default.
03Use voting to support a group decision without making it a required step.
04Hand the selected experience into Party, keeping Playlists focused on organization and selection.
03

Balancing collaboration with simplicity

A shared playlist is only useful if it makes game selection easier. Requiring every group to create a playlist, invite collaborators, and vote before each session could make a casual decision unnecessarily complex.

For that reason, the collaborative features are optional. A playlist can remain personal, become shared later, and use voting only when the group needs a structured way to decide.

01Playlists can begin as simple themed collections and become collaborative later.
02Voting is available when the group needs it, not a required step before every game.
03If the top games tie, the Party gets a short runoff between only those experiences instead of a random winner.
04The winner becomes Next Up, but the Party still confirms before Roblox launches the experience.
04

From selection to Party launch

The prototype follows the full flow from seeing friends online to opening a shared playlist, managing collaborators, choosing a game, and launching it through the existing Party.

05

Building it in Figma Make

Figma Make allowed me to move beyond static screens and test the main interactions: creating a playlist, adding and removing experiences, inviting collaborators, voting, reordering, and handing the selected game into Party.

AI-assisted prototyping made it possible to iterate quickly on those behaviors. My focus was defining when collaboration should be optional, how voting should resolve disagreement, and where the Playlist experience should end so Party can take over.

06

What I would test next

The next question is whether a shared playlist makes game selection meaningfully easier for real groups of friends.

Future testing would focus on how friends currently suggest games, whether themed collections feel distinct from Favorites, and when voting feels useful rather than too formal for a casual session.

I would also evaluate whether the runoff and final confirmation remain quick enough when a Party is already online and ready to play. The feature should support the decision without turning it into a process that needs to be managed.
