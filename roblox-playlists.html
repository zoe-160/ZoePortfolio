CASE STUDY Opportunity System Experience Prototype Reflection
01

The idea came from a pretty simple problem.

When a group of friends wants to play Roblox together, choosing the game can become its own process before anyone even starts playing. Everyone has suggestions, people are searching separately, and the group still has to figure out what everyone is actually in the mood for.

Roblox Playlists grew out of that moment. Rather than redesigning Roblox Party or creating another social system, the focus became giving friends one place to save the games they are considering, organize them, and decide what should come next.

The question I designed around was: how can friends collect game ideas and decide what to play without making the process feel like more work than the game itself?

COLLECTGive game suggestions a shared home instead of scattering them across search and conversation.
DECIDEMake voting and play order visible so the group can move forward without endless back-and-forth.
PLAYConnect the selected experience to an existing Party rather than rebuilding Roblox's social infrastructure.
02

How I wanted the playlist to work

A playlist also needed to be useful whether someone was playing alone or with friends. A user can start one privately, save experiences they want to come back to, and later turn it into a shared playlist if other people want to contribute.

Once the playlist is shared, friends can add their own suggestions, see who added each game, vote on what they want to play, and keep the list in an order that makes sense for the group. I also wanted sharing to be a choice instead of assuming every saved collection needed to become social.

01Start with a familiar collection model: create, name, save, and organize.
02Add collaboration as an optional state instead of making every playlist public by default.
03Use voting to support a decision, not to turn the playlist into a social feed.
04Hand the final choice into Party, keeping Playlists focused on planning and selection.
03

From choosing a game to actually playing it

The prototype follows the full flow from seeing that friends are online, opening a shared playlist, managing who can contribute, deciding on a game, and then launching the experience with the existing Party.

04

Building it in Figma Make

Figma Make let the project move beyond visual design into a working prototype. The main actions could actually be tested, including creating a playlist, adding and removing experiences, inviting collaborators, voting, changing the order, and moving into the Party launch.

The AI-assisted workflow helped me move through those interactions quickly, but I still had to think through who should be allowed to change what, what control belongs to the playlist owner, where privacy information should appear, and which parts of the experience should stay with Roblox Party instead of being rebuilt inside the feature.

05

What I would work on next

The main thing I would want to learn next is whether a shared playlist actually makes choosing a game easier for a real group of friends.

If I continued the project, the next step would be testing how friends currently suggest games to each other, whether voting feels useful or too formal for a casual play session, how people expect ties to work, and what would make a playlist feel meaningfully different from favorites or recently played games.

Collaboration permissions across different account settings and ages would need more attention as well. From there, the responsive version could be refined around the actions people need most when their friends are already online and they are just trying to start playing.
